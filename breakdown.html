01 · The idea

The colour is the hardness.

Polished steel changes colour as it is heated: straw, gold, bronze, purple, blue. Tempering at a lower colour leaves the steel harder; at a higher one, tougher. Fragua names each knife for the colour it is tempered to, and leaves that colour on the spine so you can see it.

The site follows the knife through the forge in order. Scroll folds a glowing bar from 2 layers to 256, with sparks on every hammer blow. Then scroll raises the temperature, and each knife holds the stage at its colour. Then scroll zooms into the edge a thousand times. The fire, the temper and the edge are all drawn in code, so the draft is finished without a single photograph.

02 · Name and mark

Fragua

Spanish for forge: the hearth and the workshop at once. Said FRAH-gwah. The four knives keep the language: Paja (straw), Oro (gold), Bronce (bronze), Azul (blue). The mark is a square billet with four layers folded inside it and one spark.

03 · The four

Four tempers, four jobs.

Drawn by js/steel.js at true relative size in millimetres. Each blade’s Damascus pattern is computed pixel by pixel from a seed (raindrop, ladder, twist, wild), so “Fold another” on a knife page draws a pattern no one has seen.

06 · Motion

Every scroll transition, and why it is there.

  1. The fold (pinned, 5.6 screens)Scroll is the hammer. The glowing bar folds seven times, 2 layers to 256; the layer count, the fold number and the heat read out as it goes. Each fold lands in the middle of its slice of scroll, so you feel the blows.
  2. Sparks and the strikeEvery fold fires a burst of sparks off the bar and jolts it down, like a hammer blow. Only going forward; scrolling back is quiet.
  3. Cooling and the etchAt the end the glow drains from the bar and the stage, the heat readout falls to room temperature, and the etched Damascus pattern comes up out of the orange.
  4. The temper (pinned, 6 screens)Scroll is the heat: 175 to 293 °C. The blade takes on the oxide colour of the moment, the stage glows with it, and a scale on the right tracks the temperature and the hardness. Each knife holds the stage at its temper, then the next one crossfades in with a small breath.
  5. The edge, 1000× (pinned, 3.6 screens)A cross-section of the Oro zooms from actual size to the apex. Labels appear at the scale where they mean something (spine, the 0.15 mm behind the edge, the 15° bevel, a hair for scale, the apex), and a live scale bar picks a round length for each zoom.
  6. El Juego, the roll (pinned, 4.4 screens)A waxed-canvas roll, rolled and strapped. The strap slides off, the canvas unrolls, and the four knives drop into their pockets one at a time, petty to cleaver, at true relative size. The list ticks off the roll and each knife as it lands, then shows the set total and “Add the set to bag”.
  7. Pictures out of the fireForge photographs come up from ember red to full colour as they scroll into view, and sink back if you scroll away.
  8. Words that riseHeadlines come up word by word from behind a mask.
  9. The knife flies between pagesDiscover from the temper stage or a card flies the drawn knife into the knife page hero and its name into the title. Knife page to knife page, the small drawing grows into the next hero.
  10. The cut (knife page, pinned)Scrolling plays the knife at its job in slow motion: Paja through a shallot, Oro through parsley, Bronce through a roast, Azul through a squash. A Higgsfield film cut into frames, with chapters, beats and a timecode; until the frames load, its two stills crossfade.
  11. Fold anotherOn a knife page, a new seed draws a new pattern and a new pattern number.
  12. Pick it upDrag any knife on a card or a knife page to turn it over; it carries on with the throw and settles face up or face down. The back shows the layers mirrored, as they run through real steel. Arrow keys flip and tilt it.
  13. Settle and stepsStop scrolling inside a pinned scene and it settles onto the nearest resting state: after each hammer blow, at each knife’s temper, at each zoom. The arrow keys and the arrows on the temper stage step between them.
  14. Smooth scroll and fast-forward linksLenis on desktop; menu links play the page past you instead of jumping.

Reduce motion (footer button, or the phone’s system setting) stops sparks, entrance effects, smooth scrolling and page transitions. Scroll-driven scenes stay, because they only move when you scroll.

08 · Pictures

34 images and 4 films, all made.

Generated on Higgsfield on 21 November 2026 for 36.48 credits, every one on the first pass (one retried after a dropped connection). Prompts live in js/shots.js with one look bible reused word for word; if a picture is ever removed, its slot falls back to a placeholder that says what goes there.

Stage 1: the four masters (side profile on the colour of their temper) and four forge scenes without a knife (fire, fold, quench, grind). Stage 2: fourteen images that pass the masters in as references: each knife at work, its spine in macro and in the hand, all four on a walnut rack, and a closing shot after service.

The masters came back close to the drawn knives: raindrop, ladder, twist and wild patterns, the handles, and the spine tints. Re-roll one with node higgsfield/generate.mjs <id> --go --force (the old file is kept).
